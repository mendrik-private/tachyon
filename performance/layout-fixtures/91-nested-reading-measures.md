# Follow the structure

An outline should reveal relationships without making each deeper explanation harder to read. The original labels, levels and supporting details remain together.

## Research notes

- Field observations record the evidence and its context.
  - Site visits connect the collected material to a particular place and time.
    - Morning observations describe the conditions before the work began.
      - Weather records retain the original measurements and the notes that explain them.
        - Instrument readings belong to the devices and methods used to collect them.
          - Calibration notes explain the reference values, the instrument settings and the checks that were performed before recording the measurements. Keep this explanation with the observations it qualifies so that another reader can understand the evidence and its limits without searching elsewhere in the document.
          - Comparison notes retain the differences between the recorded measurements and the reference values. They also identify the circumstances in which a difference was expected, so that a later review can distinguish an instrument issue from a genuine change in the observed conditions.
      - Afternoon observations document the changes after the work was completed.
  - Follow-up notes connect unresolved questions to the next review.

## Review sequence

1. Review the evidence.
   1. Inspect the original observations.
      1. Compare the documented conditions.
         1. Read the supporting explanation and retain its qualifications. A deeply nested instruction still needs a comfortable reading measure, with the original sequence, its number and its parent relationships intact.
      2. Record the remaining questions.
   2. Preserve the working notes.
