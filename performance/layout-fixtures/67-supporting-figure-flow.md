# Room for the story

Supporting imagery can share a reading column without interrupting the argument.

## A field notebook

![Botanical illustration of a flowering branch](supporting-botanical.svg)

A field notebook connects small observations to a larger account of a place. The first flowers appear along the sheltered edge of the path, where the morning light arrives before it reaches the forest floor. Their shape gives the reader a moment to pause, but the illustration is not a substitute for the observations recorded here. The text continues beside the branch while there is room, then returns to the complete reading measure below it. This change should feel like the ordinary rhythm of a printed essay, not a separate card or a second article. Each sentence remains in the same paragraph, with its original emphasis and punctuation. A reader can select across the change of width, follow the argument with the keyboard, or add a new observation without moving the figure into another column. The surface supports the writing rather than asking the author to manage its geometry.

The following paragraph continues at full width. Its separation from the first paragraph is deliberate: this is a new thought, not an artificial break added to make the picture fit. On a narrow window the illustration and the entire account stack in their original order. No description is promoted from alternative text into an invented caption.

## A separate subject

This heading clears the supporting figure. It must never be squeezed into the remaining space beside an unrelated image.

## Evidence stays separate

![Essential botanical diagram showing a measured branch](supporting-botanical.svg)

An essential diagram remains a block-level figure. Even when its dimensions match a supporting illustration, the role described by the author changes the available layouts. The engine must keep labels and evidence readable rather than treating every adjacent image as decoration.
