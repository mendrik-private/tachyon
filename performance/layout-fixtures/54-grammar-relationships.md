# Lists & relationships

Meaning chooses the marker. Space chooses the arrangement.

## Three ways to organize related content

### Unordered points

Use when the items are independent and equal.

- Support multiple devices and screen sizes.
- Keep the experience fast and responsive.
- Maintain a consistent visual language.
- Make important information easy to find.

### Labeled explanations

Use a clear label followed by its explanation.

- Performance: Optimized for speed and efficient resource use.
- Accessibility: Designed to be usable by as many people as possible.
- Maintainability: Built with clear structure and documentation.

### A real checklist

Completion is known for these tasks.

- [x] Create the project structure.
- [x] Write the first draft.
- [ ] Gather feedback.
- [ ] Refine the content and layout.

## Long-form material

### Research the topic

Gather reliable sources, take notes, and identify the key themes. Evidence deserves enough room to remain useful, including qualifications and context that do not fit in a short label.

### Write the first draft

Turn the notes into a clear structure, then focus on getting the argument onto the page. Shortening a document visually must never mean silently removing the author's words.

### Refine and improve

Edit for clarity, flow, and accuracy. Move between the wide composition and a narrow reading surface to check that every relationship remains understandable.
