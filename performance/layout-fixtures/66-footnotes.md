# An argument with its evidence

Good references let a reader follow an idea without losing the thread. Notes belong to the document, not to whichever column happens to contain them.

## 1. Keep the evidence close

A small reference should take the space of a number, not the width of its source label.[^field-observation] The surrounding sentence keeps its natural rhythm. A second source can qualify the argument without turning the paragraph into a list of destinations.[^review]

The same source may be useful more than once.[^field-observation] Its number stays the same, while a new reference receives the next number in reading order.[^method] The relationship remains clear when the window narrows or the reader enlarges the text.

Even a compact formula, $x^2 + y^2$, can share a line with a reference.[^method] Neither changes the text copied from the document. An unresolved marker such as [^missing] remains authored text; it does not acquire an invented destination.

## Notes

[^review]: **Review.** Read the evidence alongside the claim it supports. A qualification is part of the argument, not an optional decoration.

[^field-observation]: **Field observation.** Source labels can be descriptive and long. Display numbers stay compact and follow the order in which readers encounter the references.
